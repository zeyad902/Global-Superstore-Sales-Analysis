# Business Insights

## Insight 1 – Category Performance

### Observation
- Technology contributes **37.53%** of total sales and **45.23%** of total profit.
- Furniture contributes **32.52%** of total sales but only **19.44%** of total profit.

### Insight
- Technology is the strongest-performing category in both sales and profitability.
- Sales are well distributed across the three categories.
- Furniture shows relatively low profitability despite its high sales contribution.

### Recommendation
- Continue investing in Technology products.
- Investigate Furniture by analyzing discounts, shipping costs, and sub-category performance.

## Insight 2 – Top 10 Sales by Sub-Category

### Observation
- Phones is the top-selling sub-category, contributing **13.50%** of total sales.
- Copiers (**11.94%**), Chairs (**11.88%**), and Bookcases (**11.60%**) also contribute significantly.
- The top four sub-categories account for nearly **49%** of total sales.

### Insight
- A small group of sub-categories generates almost half of the company's total revenue, highlighting their importance to overall sales performance.

### Recommendation
- Prioritize inventory availability and marketing efforts for the top-performing sub-categories.
- Monitor the performance of lower-selling sub-categories to identify growth opportunities.

## Business Insight 9 – Which Products Should Be Discontinued Based on Their Sales and Profitability?

### Observation
- Identified **2,259** products with **below-average sales** and **negative profit**.
- **74% (1,670 products)** have **below-average sales volume**, while **26% (589 products)** still have **above-average sales volume**.

### Insight
- Most discontinued candidates suffer from both **low demand** and **negative profitability**, making them strong candidates for portfolio review.
- A smaller group sells in relatively high volumes but remains unprofitable, indicating issues with pricing, discounts, or product costs rather than demand.

### Recommendation
- Prioritize discontinuing or redesigning **low-volume, loss-making products**.
- Investigate **high-volume, unprofitable products** to optimize pricing, discount strategy, or costs before considering discontinuation.

## Business Insight 10 – Does Offering Higher Discounts Actually Increase Sales, or Does It Just Reduce Profit?

### Observation
- Products with **No Discount** generated the highest sales (**7.25M**) and achieved the strongest **profit margin (25.21%)**.
- As discount levels increase, **profit margins decline sharply**, turning negative for **High Discount (-17.99%)** and **Very High Discount (-75.80%)** categories.
- Although discounted products still generate sales, the additional revenue is not sufficient to offset the loss in profitability.

### Insight
- Higher discounts do **not** consistently drive higher sales, but they have a significant negative impact on profitability. The highest discount categories destroy value rather than create it.

### Recommendation
- Limit the use of **High** and **Very High** discounts.
- Apply discounts selectively to strategic products or campaigns, and monitor their impact on both sales and profit margin instead of sales alone.

## Business Insight 16 – Least Profitable Sub-Categories in Each Region

### Observation
- **Tables** is the weakest-performing sub-category in most regions, generating negative profit in **Central (-21,550.16)**, **East (-11,025.39)**, **North Asia (-5,470.57)**, **South (-27,012.36)**, **Southeast Asia (-18,618.33)**, and showing very low profitability in several other regions.
- **Machines**, **Bookcases**, **Accessories**, and **Chairs** also record losses in specific regions, indicating that profitability varies significantly by geography.
- **Southeast Asia** stands out as the weakest region, with **eight sub-categories** generating negative profit, while regions such as **Africa** and **Canada** remain profitable across all sub-categories.

### Insight
- Product profitability is highly dependent on regional market conditions. A sub-category that performs well in one region may generate losses in another.
- **Tables** consistently underperform across multiple regions, making them the highest-priority sub-category for profitability improvement.
- The concentration of loss-making sub-categories in Southeast Asia suggests pricing, discounting, or operational issues unique to that market rather than product quality alone.

### Recommendation
- Review pricing, discount policies, and logistics costs for **Tables**, particularly in the South, Central, East, and Southeast Asia regions.
- Perform region-specific profitability analysis instead of applying a global product strategy.
- Consider reducing promotions, renegotiating supplier costs, or optimizing inventory for recurring loss-making sub-categories in underperforming regions.

## Business Insight 17 – Compare Yearly Sales Growth for Each Market

### Observation
- All markets experienced overall sales growth from **2011 to 2014**, with the exception of the **US**, which recorded a **-2.83% decline in 2012** before recovering in the following years.
- **EMEA** achieved the strongest mature-market growth, with sales increasing by **47.42% in 2014** compared to 2013.
- **Canada** showed the highest single-year growth (**89.24%**) between 2011 and 2012, although this is likely influenced by its relatively small sales base.
- **APAC**, **EU**, and **LATAM** maintained consistent double-digit annual growth throughout the four-year period, indicating stable market expansion.

### Insight
- Most markets followed a healthy upward sales trajectory, suggesting sustained business expansion across regions.
- The temporary decline in the US market appears to be an isolated event rather than a long-term trend, as strong growth resumed in subsequent years.
- APAC and EU demonstrated the most balanced and sustainable growth patterns, making them reliable long-term revenue drivers.

### Recommendation
- Continue investing in high-growth markets such as **APAC**, **EU**, and **EMEA** to capitalize on their consistent expansion.
- Investigate the factors behind the **US sales decline in 2012** to reduce the risk of similar downturns.
- Monitor smaller markets such as **Canada**, where high growth percentages may reflect a low initial sales base rather than substantial market expansion.

## Business Insight 18 – Calculate the Running Total of Sales Over Time for Each Market

### Observation
- Every market shows a steadily increasing cumulative (running) sales trend from **2011 to 2014**, indicating continuous revenue accumulation over time.
- **APAC** achieved the highest cumulative sales, reaching **3.59M** by 2014, followed by **EU (2.94M)** and **US (2.30M)**.
- Although the **US** experienced a decline in annual sales during 2012, its running total continued to increase because cumulative sales never decrease.
- **Canada** recorded the lowest cumulative sales (**66.9K**) despite maintaining positive year-over-year growth.

### Insight
- The running total highlights each market's long-term contribution rather than yearly fluctuations.
- APAC, EU, and the US are the primary revenue generators, consistently building the largest cumulative sales throughout the analysis period.
- Markets with lower cumulative sales, such as Canada, contribute relatively little to total business revenue despite showing healthy growth rates.

### Recommendation
- Continue prioritizing investment in APAC and EU, as they generate the largest long-term revenue contribution.
- Use cumulative sales alongside YoY growth when evaluating market performance, since high growth percentages in small markets may not translate into significant business impact.
- Consider targeted expansion strategies for lower-contribution markets only after validating their long-term revenue potential.

## Business Insight 21 – Find Orders Where Shipping Cost Exceeds 20% of Sales

### Observation
- A significant number of orders (**2,394 orders**) have shipping costs exceeding **20% of their sales value**.
- Some extreme cases reach **48.26%** of sales, meaning nearly half of the revenue is spent on shipping.

### Insight
- High shipping costs can substantially reduce order profitability, even when sales values are relatively high.
- These orders should be reviewed, as shipping expenses may outweigh the revenue generated.

### Recommendation
- Review shipping methods and carrier contracts for these orders.
- Consider introducing minimum order values, optimized fulfillment, or customer shipping charges to reduce the impact of excessive shipping costs.
